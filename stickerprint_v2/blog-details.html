Vinyl vs. Paper Labels: Which One Should You Choose?

A practical breakdown of durability, cost, and finish for your next packaging run.

Sana MalikCreative Director · Mar 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Choosing between vinyl and paper stock is one of the first decisions any brand makes when ordering custom labels — and it shapes everything from unit cost to shelf life. Here’s how to think about the trade-offs.

Durability: where vinyl pulls ahead

Vinyl labels are inherently water, oil and UV resistant, which makes them the default choice for anything that touches moisture: beverage bottles, bath products, coolers and outdoor packaging. Paper labels, even when laminated, will eventually show wear in humid environments.

“The material is the first thing a customer touches — it sets the quality expectation before they even read the label.”

Cost: paper still wins for short runs

For runs under 250 units where the product is used indoors and quickly (candles, gift items, small-batch food with a short shelf life), coated paper stock offers a lower per-unit cost with a premium tactile finish.

Our recommendation

Default to BOPP or vinyl for anything refrigerated, outdoor, or bathroom-adjacent. Choose textured paper stock for indoor, low-moisture products where a natural, artisanal feel matters more than water resistance.
